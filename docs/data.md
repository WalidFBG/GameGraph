# Data

## Source

`C:\Data\Basketball\ValidatedData\PlayByPlay`: validated Sportradar play-by-play, one JSON file per game, read with the `OddsFactory.Sports.Data.Contracts` types. It is read only, never written.

## Reading a game

The reader walks each game's events in order and turns them into possessions, each a sequence of actions the possession tree can produce (see `possession-tree.html`). Anything it cannot read that way rejects the game, counted by reason, never silently.

1. Only `RegularSeason` games are used, for training data and for history alike. Pre-season, post-season, play-in and in-season tournament games are rejected and counted.
2. A game is used only if its validation summary reports no invalid events. Others are rejected and counted. (The game-level status field is missing in some 2025 games; the summary flag is always present.)
3. **Actions**:
   - a field-goal attempt is a two or a three, by the shooter's team;
   - a turnover, by the team of the player who committed it (a team turnover uses the event's `teamInPossession`); shot-clock violations are turnovers. A turnover that names the player who stole it (`StolenBy`) is a steal, and the ball stays live; every other turnover is a dead ball (56% are steals);
   - a `Personal` foul committed by the defence is a non-shooting foul: 2 free throws means in the bonus, 0 a side-out (the possession carries on).
4. **Shooting fouls**: a missed fouled shot is not recorded as a shot; the `Shooting` foul with 2 or 3 free throws stands in for it (a two or a three, fouled, missed). A `Shooting` foul with 1 free throw right after a made shot is an and-one (fouled, made).
5. **Free throws**: only `Regular` free throws count, and they belong to the action waiting for them, checked by the shooter's team. They are not matched by their foul's id: when a technical is called at the same moment, the shooting foul's free throws carry the technical's id.
6. **Rebounds**: only the rebound after a missed shot, or after a missed last free throw, counts. The dead-ball team rebounds after earlier free throws, and bookkeeping rebounds after a made shot, are skipped. After a miss at 0:00 the rebound counts only if play follows in the period (2.5% of missed shots at 0:00: the clock shows whole seconds); otherwise it is bookkeeping, and the miss has no rebound: the period ended it. A rebound with no catcher is a team rebound (the ball out of bounds), a dead ball: 20% of offensive rebounds and 6.5% of defensive ones.
7. **Loose-ball fouls**: a `Personal` foul while a rebound is still loose gives the rebound to the team that drew it, as a team rebound, since the foul stops play; the foul itself is then that team's non-shooting foul.
8. **Ignored**: `Technical`, `Flagrant`, `Offensive` (the turnover covers it) and `NotSet` fouls; transition take fouls (a `Personal` foul with 1 free throw: the fouled team keeps the ball); `Personal` fouls by the team with the ball; every other event (reviews, jump balls); lineup changes are read on their own (see players and lineups).
   **Free throws outside the play**: every free throw that is not `Regular`, and the `Regular` free throws of take fouls and `NotSet` fouls (about 1.5 a game), is kept by its shooter's team and attached to the next action of its period; those after a period's last action go with its last possession. Each keeps its kind (technical, flagrant, clear path, take foul, unclassified), its shooter, and for a take foul its `FoulCommittedBy`. When the team awarded a flagrant or clear-path free throw acts next against the rules (the same team twice in a row, or a possession cut short), the foul gave it the ball: the free throws go with the possession before, which records that the ball went to them, and the rules give that team the ball (`AwardedBall`). About 0.011 times a game; it recovers 52 games. The kind decides it in the data: after a flagrant to the team without the ball, the ball moved every time (50 of 50), after a technical never (0 of 1,237); the 8 games where the ball seemed to follow a take foul, which never gives the defence the ball, stay rejected as broken sequences.
   **Timeouts**: every `TeamTimeout`, with its `teamInPossession` as the team charged with it: the allowances hold (7 a game broken by 3 of 2,762 team-games, at most 4 in the fourth period never, 2 in its last 3 minutes 6 times, 2 per overtime with no carry-over). Each is attached to the next play, the next free throw of a foul, or its period's end. The `Mandatory` qualifier is not a separate kind: it marks ordinary team timeouts that met the rule of two per regulation period, inconsistently, so the graph ignores it and learns the forcing (with no timeout before 7:00 left, one follows within 30 seconds in 1,097 of 1,745 periods). The games where a team calls a timeout the rules say it does not have are rejected (about 37). 21.6% of lineup changes come at a timeout.
   **Players and lineups**: a game's players are slots, its home roster then its away roster, so the state holds plain arrays. Each action keeps its players: a shot its shooter (the `ShotBy`, or the `FoulDrawnBy` of a shooting foul that stands in for a missed fouled shot) and, if fouled, the shooting foul's `FoulCommittedBy`; a non-shooting foul its `FoulCommittedBy` and `FoulDrawnBy`; a turnover its `TurnoverCommittedBy`, if any (7,078 of 139,000 turnovers name no player, almost all shot-clock violations, and none is a steal), and whether its type is `OffensiveFoul` (15,680: always a player, never a steal; it is that player's foul, so the `Offensive` foul event is not needed); a rebound its catcher, if any (a rebound with no catcher is a team's); a made shot its `AssistedBy`, if any (63%, always a teammate); a missed shot its `BlockedBy`, if any (10%, always a defender); a steal its `StolenBy` (always a defender). A team's active players are those whose `involvement` is `Played`, `DidNotPlayCoachesDecision` or `ActiveButNotPlayed`. Each team's lineups are read from its `LineupChange` events in order (the period counted by `EndPeriod`, as for actions): the starting lineup, then each change's players off and on. They are clean: in 4,367 games every team has one starting lineup at 12:00 of the first period, of active players, and no change ever takes off a player who is not on, puts on one who is or is not active, or leaves other than five; anything else rejects the game (`InconsistentLineup`). Each team's changes are netted and attached, in event order, to the next moment the graph asks, as timeouts are: the next play or free throw, or after a period's last action the moment before it ends. A change logged after a missed last free throw and before its rebound was made before that free throw, and joins its moment (otherwise the rebounder is off court in 26 games); a change after the game's last moment rejects the game (`UnmatchedMoment`, 1 game). In event order a credited player is off court in 26 games, which are rejected (`RulesDisagreeWithData`): 10 foulers, 8 shooters (among them a buzzer three by a player the data has already taken off), 3 rebounders, 3 fouled players and 2 players who turned it over; most are late-game fouls by a player taken off seconds before. Each player's seconds played come from the changes' clocks and the period ends.
9. **The clock**: each action keeps the seconds left in its period when it happened, from the event that started it (the shot, the turnover or the foul; an and-one keeps its shot's). The data has no shot clock; the rules rebuild it.
10. **Possessions**: a possession ends with a turnover, a made shot (and its and-one), a made last free throw, a defensive rebound, or a non-shooting foul in the bonus whose last free throw is made; an offensive rebound or a side-out continues it. The next possession is the other team's.
11. **Possessions cut by the end of a period** are kept: the last action of a period's last possession need not end it (about 1 period in 10), and a miss at 0:00 may have no rebound. The graph's `PeriodRunsOut` ends them.
12. **Rejections**: an action whose team cannot be determined, a shot type that is neither two nor three, an unexpected foul (for example a `Shooting` foul with 0 free throws), a free throw nothing was waiting for, an action that interrupts another (a shot while free throws or a rebound are pending), the same team starting two possessions in a row, or a possession that ends without an ending action, unless free throws were just awarded to the team that acts (see free throws outside the play), an unknown rebound type, a period that ends while a rebound is due with time on the clock, an action whose clock is later than the one before it in its period, a difference of more than 4 possessions between the two teams, and periods 2 to 4 not starting by the alternating rule (the team that did not start the first period starts the second and third, the one that did starts the fourth: 1 game in about 4,000). About 5% of the regular-season games with valid events are rejected.
13. **Following the rules**: the reader walks each game through the graph with its observed outcomes and rejects one the rules cannot follow, for example when the rules ask for an action the data does not have (`RulesDisagreeWithData`, 98 games: 37 a timeout the team did not have left, 46 a credited player who is not on court (17 of them an awarded free throw's shooter outside the dead ball's players), 6 a player with six fouls who is not taken off before the next play, or comes back on). Training-data generation counts the action times clamped to their bound (0.32%).

## The clock in the data

Measured over the accepted regular-season games; these numbers set the graph's clock rules (`possession-tree.html`).

1. The game clock is whole seconds left in the period (`"11:46"`); `"00:00"` can mean up to a second left. There is no shot clock in the data; the rules rebuild it (24 when a possession starts, 14 after an offensive rebound, at least 14 after a side-out).
2. Against the rebuilt shot clock, about 3% of actions come late, almost all by 1 to 3 seconds, because an action is timed from the previous one while the real shot clock starts a little later (the rebound secured, the inbound touched, the tip controlled): after a made shot 6.0%, a side-out 4.3%, the period start 3.6%, an offensive rebound 2.8%, a defensive rebound 1.7%, a made free throw 1.5%, a turnover 0.6%; when the period clock binds, none. A slack of 4 seconds leaves 0.3% late (2 seconds 1.1%, 3 seconds 0.6%, 6 seconds 0.1%); those are clamped to the bound.
3. A rebound after a missed shot at 0:00 is bookkeeping (86% recorded as offensive), except in the 2.5% of missed shots at 0:00 that play follows.
4. Free throws outside the plays (technical 1.02 a game, flagrant 0.27, take-foul 0.18, clear-path 0.02) add about 1.2 points a game; without them, the score decided overtime wrongly in 136 games.

## Data GameGraph creates

Lives in `C:\Data\GameGraph\nba\`:

| Folder | What |
|---|---|
| `training-data\<node>` | Training data for one model: `examples.csv`, `games.csv` and `manifest.json` |
| `fitted-models` | Per model: `<node>.json`, the fitted model (the declaration, and per predicted parameter its intercept and each term's fit), and `<node>.training.json`, the training report (every attempt, its likelihoods, and the chosen one); both as the schemas in `schemas/` say |
| `simulations` | Simulation outputs: one CSV per simulated game, one row per simulation |

- Fitted models, reports, settings and summaries are JSON; training data is CSV to start.
- Each training-data folder has a `manifest.json`, as `schemas/training-data-manifest.schema.json` says: node, source data, date, feature names, example count, and the model the node declares. The tally (how many games were generated, short of history or rejected, by reason) is printed when the data is generated.
- `examples.csv` holds one row per example, games one after another in start-time order; `games.csv` holds one row per game (id, start time, number of examples), so examples are grouped by game without repeating the game on every row.
- Training splits by game: the latest 20% of games by start time are the validation set.
- Augmentation values are never written: they are computed in memory as the season is walked (see `plan.md`).
- Paths are set in `src/GameGraph.Host/Program.cs`.
