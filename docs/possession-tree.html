The NBA possession tree

The team-level model as built (step 13 in models.md; the measurements behind its clock rules are in data.md): every decision in a game, which distribution decides it, what its outcome is, and where each outcome leads. Click any box to jump to its details.

The idea

A game is a walk through decisions. Rules (the sport's code) keep the state, both clocks included, and say which decision comes next; models (fitted in Python, sampled in C#) decide each outcome. The same walk, with observed outcomes in place of sampled ones, writes the training data. Players are a one-way layer on top: the team state feeds them, and who is on court or who shoots changes no team decision (plan.md, step 6).

Discrete Johnson SU int: whole seconds, from 0 up to a given maximum location (identity), scale (log), skew (identity), tail (log); the maximum is given by a feature, not predicted ActionTime
Choice int: a bitmask of the chosen candidates' rows each candidate's utility (identity link, no intercept: it would cancel), and the size k given on every row; a set's probability is exp(the sum of its utilities) over the elementary symmetric polynomial of every set of k, which for k = 1 is the softmax; the features are one row per candidate Shooter, TurnedOverBy, Fouler, FouledPlayer, Rebounder, Stealer, Assister, Blocker, and the lineup nodes

Every predicted parameter is an intercept (except a choice's utility) plus its own terms, each linear or a linear spline in one feature; a node's features are those its terms and given parameters use. Training fits each node directly and along a ridge path and chooses on validation (models.md).

Time and the clock

  • The action comes first, then its time. The team picks what to do (ActionType) knowing both clocks, and only then is the time it took drawn (ActionTime), depending on the action and how the play started.
  • Time is measured from the previous action, so every second of a period is counted once, the time from a shot to its rebound included.
  • The bound is the shot clock plus 4 seconds, or the period clock if shorter. The slack covers the gap between the event an action is timed from and the real shot clock's start (the rebound secured, the inbound touched); 0.3% of observed times are above it and clamped.
  • A period ends only through PeriodRunsOut: asked at the start of a play when the period clock is the bound; a miss with 0 on the clock starts a play (whole seconds: 0:00 can mean up to a second left, and 2.5% of misses at 0:00 are followed by more play).
  • Shot-clock violations are turnovers: a turnover whose time uses up the bound.
  • Free throws take no time.
  • A choice with one outcome is still asked but never recorded: when a choice's size equals its number of candidates (a bench of one, all five off, the whole bench on) every candidate is chosen, and when its size is 0 (only the forced off leave) none is, so the engine leaves it out of the training data.

OffenceTimeout Bernoulli

Before a play or a free throw, does the team with the ball call a timeout?

Exists when
the team has a timeout left: 7 a game, at most 4 in the fourth period and 2 in its last 3 minutes, 2 in each overtime (unused regulation timeouts do not carry over)
Features
  • before a free throw rather than a play
  • whether a timeout was just called
  • for each count of the period's timeouts so far (none, one, two or more), in regulation, whether it is the count and the seconds left (a spline): the mandatory timeouts, forced at the first dead ball once none is taken before 7:00 left or fewer than two before 3:00
  • the calling team's timeouts left (a spline)
  • how the play started (see ActionTime)
  • the period
  • the score margin, and late in the game the margin (a spline)
23 features
Transition
yes → the team's timeouts left, its timeouts this period (and in the fourth period's last 3 minutes) are counted, a play after it is marked as after a timeout, and both teams are asked again; no → DefenceTimeout
Observed as
every TeamTimeout whose teamInPossession, the team charged with it, is the offence; attached to the next play, free throw or period end. 8.9 a game; the mandatory ones are ordinary team timeouts in the data

DefenceTimeout Bernoulli

Exists when
the team has a timeout left: 7 a game, at most 4 in the fourth period and 2 in its last 3 minutes, 2 in each overtime (unused regulation timeouts do not carry over)

the same, with the defence's timeouts left and without the margins and a team defensive rebound (each gained less than 0.0001): 19 features
Transition
yes → the team's timeouts left, its timeouts this period (and in the fourth period's last 3 minutes) are counted, a play after it is marked as after a timeout, and both teams are asked again; no → back to the play (AwardedFreeThrow) or the free throw (FreeThrowMade)

Substitutions Categorical

After this moment's timeouts, which teams change their five?

Exists when
before every play and every free throw, once the timeouts are done
Outcome
int: None (reference), Offence, Defence, Both
Features
  • before a free throw, and between free throws
  • whether a timeout was called at this moment
  • the period, its seconds left (a spline), late in the game
  • how the play started: after the period start, a made shot, a dead-ball turnover, a side-out, or a team rebound at either end (against every other start)
  • each five's longest stint, the log of its seconds plus one (a spline)
  • whether each team must change: a fouled-out player is on court and the bench has someone to bring on, before a play with time left
  • how many of the defence's five are in foul trouble (the offence's gained less than 0.0001)
17 features
Transition
a team that must change changes whatever the outcome; each changing team in turn, the offence first → SubstitutionCount (a team with no one left on its bench cannot change, and is passed over); then the moment carries on to AwardedFreeThrow or FreeThrowMade
Observed as
the lineup changes in event order, netted per team over the moment they come before: the next play or free throw, or after a period's last action, the moment before it ends. Why no rule
substitutions need a dead ball, but 0.3 to 0.6% of moments after a live start change without a timeout: a dead ball inside the play (the ball out of bounds off a deflection, a kicked ball) that the graph does not model

AwardedFreeThrow Categorical

Before the play, is a free throw outside the play awarded, of which kind, and to whom?

Outcome
int: None (reference); a technical, flagrant or take-foul free throw to the offence or the defence; a clear-path one to the offence (the kinds and teams that occur)

  • late in the game (the fourth period or later, at most 120 seconds left)
  • whether the team with the ball is at home

Transition

Observed as

AwardedShooter Choice

Outcome

ActionType Categorical

What does the team with the ball do?

Outcome
int: TwoPointAttempt (reference), ThreePointAttempt, Turnover, NonShootingFoul
Features
  • team points per possession (context)
  • the defence's team fouls
  • seconds left on the shot clock and the period clock
  • late in the game (the fourth period or later, at most 120 seconds left), and there the score margin (a spline) and the seconds left (a spline)
  • for each late margin band (trailing by 3; leading by 1 to 3, 4 to 8, 9 to 12), whether the play is in it and its seconds left (a spline): a trailing defence fouls to stop the clock, from about 30 seconds when down 1 to 3 and about a minute when down 4 to 8, and gives up from 11
  • after an offensive rebound, a team rebound at either end, or a steal (against every other start): after a team rebound only 51% (offensive) and 61% (defensive) of actions are shots, against about 75%
  • the five on court: the offence five's two-point attempts, three-point attempts and fouls drawn per action, and the defence five's steals per action, each summed (splines): the five sets the twos/threes mix
23 features; the other play starts, the score margin, at home, after a timeout and whether the clock binds each gained less than 0.0001 in validation and were dropped
Transition
→ ActionTime, which then follows the action
Observed as
the next action: a field-goal attempt by its points, a turnover, a non-shooting foul

ActionTime Discrete Johnson SU · truncated

How many seconds did the action take?

Outcome
int: whole seconds, from 0 up to the bound
Features
  • whether the play follows a timeout, and late in the game (the ball is advanced: 8 seconds after a made shot against 13)
  • the chosen action: three, turnover, foul (against a two), and a foul late in the game (these come fast: 57% within 3 seconds when the defence trails, against 28% at other times)
  • how the play started: after an offensive rebound, a team offensive rebound, a team defensive rebound, a made shot, a made free throw, a steal, a dead-ball turnover, a side-out, the ball after awarded free throws or the period start (against a player's defensive rebound): the next action comes 6.5 seconds after a steal against 13.4 after a dead-ball turnover; every play start but the reference has its own indicator, so a new one joins them
  • the bound (a spline on location and scale), its log (skew and tail), whether the period clock binds
  • score margin (a spline on location and scale), late in the game, at home
Transition
both clocks run down by the outcome, and the five on court are read at the new clock; then two or three → Shooter; turnover → TurnoverKind; non-shooting foul → adds one to the defence's team fouls (and to its fouls in the last two minutes when at most 120 seconds are left) → Fouler
Observed as
the period clock at the previous action minus the clock at this one, clamped to the bound
