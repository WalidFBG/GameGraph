The idea

A game is a walk through decisions. Rules (the sport's code) keep the state, both clocks included, and say which decision comes next; models (fitted in Python, sampled in C#) decide each outcome. The same walk, with observed outcomes in place of sampled ones, writes the training data. It is player-agnostic: choosing players waits for substitutions.

Every predicted parameter is an intercept plus its own terms, each linear or a linear spline in one feature; a node's features are those its terms and given parameters use. Training fits each node directly and along a ridge path and chooses on validation (models.md).

Time and the clock

  • The action comes first, then its time. The team picks what to do (ActionType) knowing both clocks, and only then is the time it took drawn (ActionTime), depending on the action and how the play started.
  • Time is measured from the previous action, so every second of a period is counted once, the time from a shot to its rebound included.
  • The bound is the shot clock plus 4 seconds, or the period clock if shorter. The slack covers the gap between the event an action is timed from and the real shot clock's start (the rebound secured, the inbound touched); 0.3% of observed times are above it and clamped.
  • A period ends only through PeriodRunsOut: asked at the start of a play when the period clock is the bound; a miss with 0 on the clock starts a play (whole seconds: 0:00 can mean up to a second left, and 2.5% of misses at 0:00 are followed by more play).
  • Shot-clock violations are turnovers: a turnover whose time uses up the bound.
  • Free throws take no time.

AwardedFreeThrow Categorical

Before the play, is a free throw outside the play awarded, and to whom?

Outcome
int: None (reference), Offence, Defence
Features
  • score margin
  • late in the game (the fourth period or later, at most 120 seconds left)
Transition
offence or defence → AwardedFreeThrowMade, then asked again; none → PeriodRunsOut if the period clock is the bound, otherwise ActionType
Observed as
technical, flagrant and clear-path free throws, and those of take fouls (about 1.5 a game), each attached to the next play of its period, or asked before the period ends after its last play

PeriodRunsOut Bernoulli · gated

Does the period run out before anything else happens?

Exists when
a play starts with the period clock below the shot clock plus 4 seconds; a miss that leaves 0 on the clock starts a play
Features
  • seconds left in the period
  • score margin
  • which period
  • whether it follows a miss
Transition
yes → PeriodEnds; no → ActionType, or OffensiveRebound after a miss
Observed as
yes when the period has no further action

What does the team with the ball do?

Outcome
int: TwoPointAttempt (reference), ThreePointAttempt, Turnover, NonShootingFoul
Features
  • score margin
  • team scoring rate (context)
  • the defence's team fouls

→ ActionTime, which then follows the action

ActionTime Discrete Johnson SU · truncated

How many seconds did the action take?

Outcome
int: whole seconds, from 0 up to the bound
Features
  • the chosen action: three, turnover, foul (against a two)
  • how the play started: after an offensive rebound, a made shot, a made free throw, a turnover, a side-out or the period start (against a defensive rebound)
  • the bound (a spline on location and scale), its log (skew and tail), whether the period clock binds
  • score margin (a spline on location and scale), late in the game
Transition
both clocks run down by the outcome; then two or three → FouledOnShot; turnover → EndOfPossession; non-shooting foul → InBonus
Observed as
the period clock at the previous action minus the clock at this one, clamped to the bound

→ ShotMade; a foul adds one to the defence's team fouls

ShotMade Bernoulli

Does the shot go in?

Features
  • score margin
  • team scoring rate (context)
  • shot type
  • fouled on the shot
Transition
score +2 or +3 if made; fouled → FreeThrowsDue; made → EndOfPossession; missed → OffensiveRebound
Observed as

InBonus Bernoulli

Does this non-shooting foul give free throws?

Features
  • the defence's team fouls this period, this one included
  • which period
Transition
adds one to the defence's team fouls; yes → FreeThrowsDue (2 free throws); no → side-out, shot clock to at least 14 seconds, a new play
Observed as
whether the non-shooting foul was followed by free throws
Why a model
the real bonus is a rule, but the tree does not generate every team foul, so a counter of its own fouls would run low; the model learns the step from the fouls it sees.

Not in the tree yet

  • Home advantage: no feature says which team is at home, so simulated margins lean 2.4 points less to the home team than real ones
  • The ball after a flagrant or a take foul: the fouled team keeps it in reality, not in a simulation
  • Players: who shoots, rebounds, turns it over and is fouled, together with substitutions
  • Competing risks: one clock per kind of action, the earliest wins, so time and action decide each other
  • A continuous time, once the data has tenths of a second
  • Foul types, turnover types (live-ball steals and their fast breaks), shot zones, assists, blocks, timeouts, late-game fouling
