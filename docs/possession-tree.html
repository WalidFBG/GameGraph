The NBA possession tree

A proposal for the next model, at team level: every decision in a possession, which model family decides it, what its outcome is, and where each outcome leads. Click any box to jump to its details.

The idea

Today a possession is one yes/no decision. This tree splits it into the decisions that happen on court, chosen so that the engine gets something it does not have yet: new model families, new outcome types, new parameter layouts, a node that exists only in some states, and rules with their own state. It is player-agnostic: choosing players waits for substitutions, so that "the five on court" is something the simulation decides itself.

The tree

The next step: the tree without the clock

The first step builds every decision except the clock. Possessions still follow the real game's schedule (who has the ball and how many possessions each period has), as today; the clock replaces that in the step after.

Fouls and free throws come in this step, not later: about one possession in eight has free throws (8.6% have only free throws, 3.9% a shot and free throws). Most are a fouled missed shot, which the data records as a shooting foul with 2 or 3 free throws and no shot, exactly the tree's shot, fouled, missed.

  • Families: Categorical (ActionType) and Bernoulli (the other five decisions). The Count family waits for the clock.
  • How the data maps: a shooting foul with 2 or 3 free throws and no shot is a two or a three, fouled and missed; a made shot with a shooting foul and 1 free throw is fouled and made (an and-one); a non-shooting foul with free throws is a non-shooting foul in the bonus; one without free throws is a side-out.
  • The bonus is learnt, not ruled: InBonus decides whether a non-shooting foul gives free throws, from the defence's team fouls. An exact rule would need every team foul, and the tree does not generate them all (loose-ball and away-from-the-ball fouls, for example), so its count would run low and disagree with the data; the last-two-minutes rule would also need the clock.
  • Features without the clock: the clock features (seconds left on the shot clock and the period clock) come with the clock step; this step uses the score, the period, team fouls and the context.

All three take one feature vector per decision. The choice shape (one feature vector per candidate) arrives with player-level nodes.

Time and the clock

  • The action comes first, then its time. The team knows the score, the period and both clocks, picks what to do (ActionType), and only then is the time it took drawn (ActionTime), depending on the action: turnovers come quickly, threes after more passing.
  • Time never exceeds the clock. ActionTime is truncated at the clock that binds, the shorter of the shot clock and the period clock. In the data, an action's time is always within the clock, so the truncated model matches it exactly.
  • Shot-clock violations are turnovers. The data records them as turnovers of type ShotClockViolation (about 1.3 per game), so they need no special node: a turnover whose time uses up the shot clock.
  • The period can only run out once the shot clock is off. While the shot clock is shorter than the period clock, it forces an action or a violation first. Only when the period clock is the shorter one does PeriodRunsOut exist, so the period can never run out mid-quarter.
  • Whole seconds. The data's game clock has whole seconds only, with no tenths in the last minute, and many actions share a second, so a time of 0 is common. A count family allows that; a continuous duration would need interval likelihoods for no gain.

A possible build order

Two steps. Fouls and free throws come with the first, because about one possession in eight has free throws and the data cannot be read without them.

StepAddsBrings
1The tree without the clock: ActionType, FouledOnShot, ShotMade, InBonus, FreeThrowsAwarded, FreeThrowMade, OffensiveRebound; possessions still follow the real schedulethe Categorical family, rules with their own state (team fouls), loops inside a possession
2ClockCheck, PeriodRunsOut, ActionTime, PeriodEnds, and the clock featuresthe Count family, truncation, a gated node; removes the schedule leak

Not in the tree yet

  • Players: who shoots, rebounds, turns it over and is fouled (the Choice family and its feature shape), together with substitutions
  • Competing risks: one clock per kind of action, the earliest wins, so time and action decide each other
  • A continuous time (double), once the data has tenths of a second
  • Foul types (offensive, loose-ball, technical, flagrant), turnover types (live-ball steals and their fast breaks), shot zones
  • Assists, blocks, timeouts, late-game fouling, jump balls, end-of-period heaves, coach's challenges
